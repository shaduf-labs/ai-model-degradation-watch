How we read · updated 18 September 2026

Alert on the task symptom. Then investigate the cause.

A model name is not an outcome, and a status page is not a task replay. The receipt has to connect the work a person expected with the work that survived.

New diagnostic: no alert without a user-visible symptom, a baseline, a threshold, and a named reversible action.

The alert contract

FieldMinimum recordWhy it matters
Task and surfaceFixed task, product, client, region, plan, and time window.Defines whose work changed and where.
Baseline and thresholdPrior outcome, sample, acceptable range, and trigger.Stops a vivid anecdote becoming an incident.
Identity and routeRequested model, provider-served model, fallback, service tier, capacity state.Separates model, route, entitlement, and rollout changes.
Context and toolsPrompt, prior context, instructions, effort, tool calls, retries, and completion boundary.Separates context, orchestration, and tool failures.
Outcome and economicsFinal artifact, self-check, corrections, elapsed time, allowance, meter, cost, and ledger status.Joins quality to the usable work window.
ActionRetry, wait, pin, switch, replay, or no action; include a kill rule.Turns a watch into something an operator can use.

Evidence ladder

  1. Provider records: declared incidents, releases, access rules, limits, and postmortems.
  2. Matched replays: same task, surface, model identity, settings, context, tools, and artifact rubric.
  3. Runtime and account telemetry: route, context, cache, tool lifecycle, allowance, and outcome; a meter without outcome is not quality.
  4. Benchmarks: task set, harness, effort, tool stack, call budget, artifact rubric, cost, and date.
  5. Community reports: early warning and audience language; selection blocks prevalence and causality.

What would change the call?

Replicate the same task across affected and control accounts or providers; capture the served model and route; hold context and tools stable; score the completed artifact and correction burden; join client cost to provider ledger; and alert only when the threshold changes a real decision. A cross-model capability verdict still needs repeated negative movement after availability, routing, product, safety, access, and context explanations are checked.
